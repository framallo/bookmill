# The Garden That Kept Itself

By summer the gardener no longer woke at dawn. The garden turned without her — every crooked key drinking the same wind, every flower keeping its own time, the whole field moving at once for the first time in her long life.

She sat on the hill and watched it breathe. It was not that she had stopped caring for the garden. It was that she had stopped trying to be the garden.

"There it is," she said. "The better way was never a bigger hand. It was a smaller key, and trust."

And the wind, for once, agreed.
