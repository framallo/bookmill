# The Still Garden

Once there was a garden where nothing grew by itself. Every flower, every vine, every blade of grass had a tiny brass key in its root, and each morning the old gardener walked the rows and wound them, one by one, so the garden could turn through another day.

The gardener was slow, and the garden was large. By the time she reached the last row, the first row had already wound down and gone still. She never saw the whole garden moving at once. She only ever saw the part she had just touched.

"There must be a better way," she said to the wind. The wind, as winds do, said nothing useful back.
